I want to thank the editors and the staff of Sports Illustrated for this tremendous honor.

When I look back at the past century, I realize how blessed I've been. I grew up in Louisville, Kentucky, in a time of segregation. I remember not being allowed to eat in certain restaurants because of the color of my skin. But those experiences made me stronger. They forced me to look deeper inside myself, to find the courage and the belief that would carry me through the battles of my life—both inside and outside the boxing ring.

People often ask me what I am most proud of. Is it my three heavyweight championship titles? Is it the Olympic gold medal? Is it the fame, the money, the travels around the world?

No. What I am most proud of is my belief in freedom, justice, and equality.

There were times when I made mistakes. Nobody is perfect. But I always tried to do what I believed was right for my people and for humanity. I stood up for my convictions, and I paid a price for them. I lost my title, I lost my prime years in the ring, and I faced severe criticism. But I would do it all over again, because freedom is more important than a boxing career, and human dignity is more valuable than any championship belt.

Today, as I look at the world, I see so much work left to do. We still face hatred, prejudice, and division. But I also see hope in the eyes of the young people. You are the future. Never let anyone tell you that you cannot make a difference. Stand up for what you believe in, treat others with kindness, and never give up.

Thank you for this incredible honor, and God bless you all.
